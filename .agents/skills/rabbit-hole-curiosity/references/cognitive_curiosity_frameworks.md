# Cognitive Curiosity Frameworks & Question Bank

This reference manual codifies the six psychological, epistemological, and mathematical frameworks that power the **Rabbit-Hole Curiosity** skill.

---

## 1. George Loewenstein's Information-Gap Theory & Ian Leslie's Epistemic Curiosity

- **Information-Gap Theory (Loewenstein, 1994)**: Curiosity arises when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge. Crucially, the intensity of curiosity is proportional to the *salience of the gap*: a person who knows nothing about a subject feels zero curiosity, whereas a person who spots a small, puzzling anomaly inside a familiar frame experiences an irresistible "cognitive itch."
- **Diversive vs. Epistemic Curiosity (Berlyne, 1960; Leslie, *Curious*, 2014)**:
  - *Diversive curiosity* is attraction to whatever is new or flashy at the moment (restless clicking that never builds depth).
  - *Epistemic curiosity* is the sustained, effortful quest for deep causal understanding, historical context, and structural mastery.

**Agent Question Prompts**:
1. *What small contradiction or surprising detail in this topic creates an immediate information gap?*
2. *What do 95% of casual observers assume is simple about this topic that is actually deeply complex when inspected closely?*

---

## 2. Abductive Reasoning & the Venatic Clue Paradigm (Peirce, Doyle, Ginzburg)

- **Abduction / Retroduction (Charles Sanders Peirce; Eco & Sebeok, *The Sign of Three*, 1983)**:
  - *Deduction* proves that something *must* be (`Rule + Case -> Result`).
  - *Induction* shows that something *actually is* operative (`Case + Result -> Rule`).
  - *Abduction* conjectures what *may* be (`Anomalous Result + Proposed Hidden Rule -> Case`). Every scientific discovery and detective breakthrough begins with abduction.
- **The Venatic (Hunter's) Paradigm (Carlo Ginzburg, *Clues, Myths, and the Historical Method*, 1989)**:
  - Instead of looking only at grand averages, the investigator examines seemingly negligible marginalia—etymological shifts, forgotten footnotes, edge-case bugs, or anomalous artifacts—as "tracks" left by a deeper historical or systemic force.

**Agent Question Prompts**:
1. *If the current state of this topic is the "crime scene," what sequence of past events had to happen to produce this exact footprint?*
2. *What "trifle" or footnote in the history of this topic is actually the key to the entire design?*

---

## 3. Rhizomatic Burrows & Associative Trails (Deleuze & Guattari, Bush, Nelson, Borges)

- **The Rhizome vs. the Tree (Deleuze & Guattari, *A Thousand Plateaus*, 1980)**:
  - Hierarchical ("arborescent") knowledge forces every concept into a rigid parent-child taxonomy.
  - A **rhizome** (modeled explicitly on an underground animal **burrow** with multiple entryways) allows any node to connect directly to any other node across disciplines.
- **Associative Indexing & Hypertext Trails (Vannevar Bush, *"As We May Think"*, 1945; Ted Nelson, *"The Hypertext"*, 1965)**:
  - The mind navigates by *trails of association* rather than alphabetical filing cabinets.

**Agent Question Prompts**:
1. *If we follow two hypertext hops away from this concept, what surprising bridge connects it back to the original problem?*
2. *What are the "multiple entryways" into this topic (mathematical, historical, biological, aesthetic, economic)?*

---

## 4. Lateral Hole-Digging & the Nine-Dot Box (Edward de Bono, Sam Loyd, J.P. Guilford)

- **Digging Holes vs. Switching Holes (Edward de Bono, *The Use of Lateral Thinking*, 1967)**:
  - *"Logic is the tool that is used to dig holes deeper and bigger, to make them altogether better holes. But if the hole is in the wrong place, then no amount of improvement is going to put it in the right place... Vertical thinking is digging the same hole deeper; lateral thinking is trying again elsewhere."*
- **The 1914 Nine-Dot Puzzle (Sam Loyd, *Cyclopedia of 5000 Puzzles*, p. 301; James L. Adams, *Conceptual Blockbusting*, 1974)**:
  - Human perception automatically draws an imaginary boundary around the outer 8 dots of a $3 \times 3$ grid. "Thinking outside the box" means identifying the **unspoken constraint** that nobody actually required, and extending the line of inquiry beyond it.

**Agent Question Prompts**:
1. *What is the imaginary $3 \times 3$ box that everyone in this conversation is unconsciously obeying?*
2. *Where are we digging an old hole deeper when we should be starting a lateral shaft in a new location?*

---

## 5. Bisociation & "Thinking Aside" (Arthur Koestler, *The Act of Creation*, 1964)

- **Routine Association vs. Bisociation**:
  - Routine thought moves along a single associative plane or "matrix" ($M_1$) governed by fixed rules of the game.
  - **Bisociation** occurs when a problem or concept ($L$) is simultaneously perceived at the intersection of two independent, previously unrelated matrices ($M_1 \times M_2$).

**Agent Question Prompts**:
1. *What completely unrelated field (e.g., mycology, medieval siege craft, counterpoint music, double-entry bookkeeping) solves an isomorphic problem?*
2. *What happens when we import the governing laws of Matrix B into Matrix A?*

---

## 6. Hofstadter Stack Management & Mertonian Serendipity vs. Apophenia

- **Pushing and Popping the Inquiry Stack (Douglas Hofstadter, *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, 1979)**:
  - Every rabbit-hole tangent is a `PUSH` onto a recursive mental stack. Productive curiosity always remembers to `POP` back up, carrying the subterranean insight back to illuminate the surface question.
- **Serendipity vs. Apophenia (Robert K. Merton, *The Travels and Adventures of Serendipity*, 2004; Umberto Eco, *Foucault's Pendulum*, 1988)**:
  - *Serendipity* is observing an unexpected, **verifiable** anomalous datum and using sagacity to build a valid new theory.
  - *Apophenia* (hermetic drift) is mistaking superficial verbal or numerical rhymes for causal connections. Always test every subterranean thread against primary evidence.
